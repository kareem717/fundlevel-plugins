# Talk track — Routing handoff (with back-pocket offer)

The default LinkedIn talk track for brand-side outbound. Use after a connection request is
accepted, when you're trying to figure out who handles creator/sponsorship partnerships at
a company and hand them a shortlist as an opening artifact.

## When to use this

Use when **all** of these are true:

- You've sent a cold or near-cold connection request that the recipient accepted.
- The recipient's company has run *some* form of creator/influencer/dev-marketing work
  before — you have a specific, verifiable example to cite (a creator they sponsored, a
  campaign they ran, a partnership announcement).
- Your goal is to qualify the right contact and hand off a creator shortlist as a soft
  open — not to book a meeting.

Don't use it when:

- The company has never done any creator/influencer work — there's no proof-of-homework to
  cite. Use a different talk track (cold-no-signal, when written).
- You're already in an active deal with the company — use the AE workflow, not cold DMs.
- You're reaching out to a creator (not a brand) — that's a different track.

## The tree at a glance

```
Connection accepts
       │
       ▼
   Stage 1: Routing question (Branch A — proof-of-homework opener)
       │
       ├── "me!" / "yes that's me" ──────────────────►  Branch B: Pivot to back-pocket offer
       │                                                       │
       │                                                       ▼
       │                                                Bridge to email → done
       │
       └── "actually, talk to [colleague]" ──────────►  Branch C: Redirect chain
                                                              │
                                                              ├── C1: Reply to original
                                                              │       prospect — thank
                                                              │       them + ask for the
                                                              │       colleague's email
                                                              │
                                                              └── C2: Send warm connection
                                                                      request to colleague
                                                                      → run Stage 1 + Branch
                                                                      B on them
```

## Branch A — Stage 1: routing question (post-connection-acceptance)

Send as **3–4 short message bubbles**, not one block. Total length under ~50 words.

The structure:

1. First-name greeting
2. "Glad to be connected" (optional 💪 / 🙏 / no emoji)
3. The **specific proof-of-homework** observation
4. The **routing question**
5. Light gratitude close

Two example phrasings (don't copy verbatim — vary based on the specific signal):

**Example 1 — when the prospect's company has named-creator sponsorships:**

> Hey Ian!
>
> Glad to be connected 💪
>
> I noticed you guys ran a few collabs earlier this year with folks like Theo.
>
> Curious; do you know who was responsible for handling those partnerships at posthog?
>
> thanks in advance^

**Example 2 — when the signal is a specific campaign or content asset, not a creator
name:**

> Hey [first]!
>
> Glad to be connected
>
> Saw the partnership y'all did with [creator/campaign] over the past few months — really
> well executed.
>
> Quick one — is partnerships still owned out of your team, or is there someone else I
> should be chatting with?
>
> thanks^

Notes on the copy:

- **Lowercase mid-sentence is fine** — "curious;" or "saw the…" or "thanks^" all match
  the LinkedIn-native voice.
- **`^` instead of period** on closing line is a small tic that reads conversational.
- **"Curious; do you know who…"** beats "Curious, who handles…" — the semicolon + "do you
  know who" softens the question even further.
- **Use "folks like" or "guys like"** before naming the creator — keeps the example
  specific without sounding exhaustive.

## Branch B — they say they're the right person

When they reply with "me!" or "I handle that" or any confirmation, **pivot to the
back-pocket offer**.

Send as 2–3 short bubbles.

The structure:

1. Enthusiastic acknowledgement ("oh, awesome!" / "amazing")
2. Optional: brief proof-of-homework callback if the original was Branch C2 (referral-led)
3. **Back-pocket offer** of the creator shortlist deliverable
4. **Soft CTA** ("worth a peek?" / "would you be against me forwarding…")
5. *Wait for a yes*
6. **Email bridge** — ask for the email even if you already have it

Two example phrasings:

**Example 1 — direct pivot after Branch A confirmation:**

> oh, awesome!
>
> I actually wanted to forward a shortlist (analytics, URLs, etc.) of a couple creators in
> our network — I think they'd do a really nice job showcasing [company].
>
> Do you think it's worth a peek? doesn't hurt to have them in your back pocket :)

After they say yes:

> Amazing — is there an email I can send the google sheet to?

**Example 2 — pivot with the "totally against" framing (use when they haven't yet
indicated openness):**

> curious; would you be totally against me forwarding a couple profiles (analytics, etc.)
> your way?
>
> I think they'd kill it for you guys and I doubt it'd hurt to have em in your back
> pocket :)

After they say yes:

> Amazing — is there an email I can drop the sheet into?

Notes on the copy:

- **"Doesn't hurt to have them in your back pocket :)"** is the load-bearing line. Vary
  the wording, keep the mechanic. ("I doubt it'd hurt to have em on hand", "good to have
  in your back pocket either way", etc.)
- **"Would you be totally against…"** sets up a no-as-default frame — agreeing is easier
  than disagreeing.
- **Name the artifact concretely** — "shortlist", "google sheet", "couple of profiles".
  Vague offers ("some research") get vague responses.
- **Always ask for the email** — even if you already have it from Apollo or CRM. The act
  of them sharing it makes your follow-up a warm email by definition. If they say "send
  it here" (in DMs), send it in DMs but ask for a better email after they've reviewed.

## Branch C — they redirect to a colleague

The reply will look like:

> "Belle let me know I should talk to you" *(this is the colleague side, after redirect)*
>
> or
>
> "Actually, you'll want to talk to [colleague name] — they handle that"

When you're the one *being* redirected (you got the "talk to X" reply), run **both
branches in parallel**.

### Branch C1 — reply to the original prospect

Same thread. One message bubble, two messages max.

The structure:

1. Brief gratitude
2. Ask for the colleague's preferred contact (try to get an email, not just "introduce me")

Example:

> oh awesome — thanks for pointing me in the right direction!
>
> what would be the best place to contact them? is that an email I could use?

Notes on the copy:

- **"What's the best place to contact them"** is broader than asking for an email
  directly — it gives them room to say "Slack" or "they handle it via [tool]". Then the
  follow-up "is that an email I could use?" narrows back to the answer you want.
- **Don't promise to follow up with the original prospect later.** The relationship is
  done unless they re-engage; padding the message with "I'll let you know how it goes"
  invites cleanup work that never converts.

### Branch C2 — open a thread with the new prospect

Two-step process.

**Step C2.1 — connection request with a name-drop note:**

Send a connection request. In the note (LinkedIn allows up to 300 characters but you only
need 5–8 words), use a **lowercase, casual** referrer name-drop:

> `belle told me about you :)`

Or:

> `chatted with belle about influencer collabs @ aws — she said i should reach out :)`

Notes on this connection-request copy:

- **All lowercase**, deliberately — signals peer-to-peer, not corporate.
- **First name only** for the referrer.
- **Single trailing `:)`** — anything more reads promotional. Don't use 💪 or other emojis
  in connection-request notes; save them for post-acceptance DMs.
- **Don't pitch in the note.** The note exists to get the connection accepted; the pitch
  (or routing-then-offer) happens after acceptance.

**Step C2.2 — once they accept, open with referral framing + proof-of-homework:**

Different from Branch A's opener — the proof-of-homework is preceded by the referrer
name-drop:

> Hey Margot!
>
> Glad to be connected
>
> Belle let me know I should talk to you.
>
> I saw some of the work you guys did with folks like bycloud over the last year and
> wanted to reach out —
>
> curious; would you be totally against me forwarding a couple profiles (analytics, etc.)
> your way? I think they'd kill it for you guys and I doubt it'd hurt to have em in your
> back pocket :)

Note this version compresses Branch A + Branch B into a single set of bubbles, because the
warm-referral context lets you skip the explicit routing question. The recipient already
knows they're the right person (the referrer named them).

After they say yes, bridge to email same as Branch B:

> Amazing — is there an email I can send the google sheet to?

If they say "share it here that's perfect" — share it in DMs, then ask for an email after
they've reviewed (so the next thread isn't in LinkedIn).

## Things that go wrong with this talk track

- **You don't actually have a proof-of-homework example.** Cited generic ones ("you do
  influencer marketing") show as templated and reply rate craters. If you don't have a
  specific example, don't run this talk track — pick a different one or do more research.
- **You pitch in Stage 1 instead of asking the routing question.** Same problem as cold
  email — "want to chat about our creator network?" before they've shown any interest is
  a hard pass.
- **The back-pocket offer becomes a meeting ask.** "Want to jump on a 15-min call?"
  destroys the optionality framing. Stay deliverable-shaped until they ask for time
  themselves.
- **You skip the email bridge.** Long LinkedIn DM threads die. Get the asset transfer onto
  email by Stage 3 at the latest.
- **You ghost the original prospect after a redirect.** Always close Branch C1. It costs
  one message and either gets you a warm email for the colleague or surfaces a "they
  prefer Slack" you'd otherwise miss.
- **You use Branch A's opener in a Branch C2 thread, or vice versa.** A warm-referral
  thread should *lead* with the referrer name-drop; a cold thread shouldn't fake one.

## Quick reference: copy variants by stage

For when you're running the track and want to vary phrasing across multiple prospects in
the same session.

**Greeting bubble (Stage 1):**

- `Hey [first]!`
- `Hi [first]!`
- `Hey [first] 👋` *(use sparingly)*

**"Glad to be connected" bubble:**

- `Glad to be connected`
- `Glad to be connected 💪`
- `Thanks for connecting`
- *(omit if the connection was clearly warm — go straight to the substance)*

**Proof-of-homework openers:**

- `I noticed you guys ran a few collabs earlier this year with folks like [creator]`
- `Saw the partnership y'all did with [creator/campaign] over the past few months`
- `I saw some of the work you guys did with folks like [creator] over the last year`
- `Caught the [campaign name] launch — really well done`

**Routing questions:**

- `Curious; do you know who was responsible for handling those partnerships at [company]?`
- `Quick one — is partnerships still owned out of your team, or is there someone else I
  should be chatting with?`
- `Are you the right person to talk to about that, or should I be reaching out elsewhere?`

**Back-pocket offers:**

- `I wanted to forward a shortlist (analytics, URLs, etc.) of a couple creators in our
  network — I think they'd do a really nice job showcasing [company]. Doesn't hurt to
  have them in your back pocket :)`
- `would you be totally against me forwarding a couple profiles (analytics, etc.) your
  way? I think they'd kill it for you guys and I doubt it'd hurt to have em in your back
  pocket :)`
- `we put together a shortlist of creators we think would land really well for [company]
  — happy to share if useful, no strings`

**Email bridges:**

- `Amazing — is there an email I can send the google sheet to?`
- `Perfect — what's the best email for the asset?`
- `Great — where should I send it?`

**Connection-request notes (Branch C2.1):**

- `belle told me about you :)`
- `chatted with belle re: influencer collabs @ [company] — she said i should reach out :)`
- `belle pointed me your way — hoping to connect :)`

**C1 follow-up to original prospect:**

- `oh awesome — thanks! what's the best place to contact them? is that an email I could
  use?`
- `appreciate that — do you know if [colleague] is reachable on email? happy to send
  there`
